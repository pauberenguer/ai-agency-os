# Client Onboarding and Fulfillment

## Overview

Churn is decided in the first 30 days. A client who sees a concrete result early forgives a slow month later; a client who waits three weeks for a kickoff call is mentally gone before the work starts, regardless of what the results eventually look like.

The two levers are **speed to first value** and **communication cadence**. Most agencies under-invest in both and then blame results for churn that was actually caused by silence.

## When to Use

- A deal just closed and delivery needs to start
- Churn is high or clients are disengaged
- Building repeatable SOPs for delivery
- Preparing a QBR or renewal conversation
- A client relationship is deteriorating

Don't use for: the sales process (use `sales-call-framework`) or CRM automation build (use `crm-pipeline-ops`).

## The First 30 Days

**Day 0 — the moment of signature.** Momentum peaks here and decays immediately.

- Welcome email within 15 minutes: what happens next, when, and who to contact
- Onboarding form and asset-request list sent immediately
- Kickoff call booked **within 48 hours** — not "sometime next week"
- Invoice and payment method set up
- Client added to the shared communication channel

**Days 1–3 — kickoff.**

- Confirm goals, and translate them into numbers both sides agree on
- Set the definition of success at 30, 60, and 90 days
- Collect access: ad accounts, analytics, CRM, domain, social profiles, brand assets
- Confirm the single point of contact on each side
- Agree the communication cadence and where it happens
- Walk through the delivery timeline week by week

**Access collection is the top blocker.** Send a written checklist with exact instructions for each platform, and follow up daily until complete. Every day without access is a day of the client's paid month burning with nothing to show.

**Days 4–14 — deliver something visible.**

Ship one concrete thing the client can see, well before the full engagement is running. An audit with three specific findings, the missed-call text-back turned on, the Google Business Profile fixed, the first ad live. **The purpose is proof of motion**, and it is worth more to retention than a perfect strategy document.

**Days 15–30 — first results and the first real report.**

- The first meaningful metric movement, however small
- A report the client can actually read (see `growth-metrics-and-reporting`)
- A 30-day check-in call: what has been done, what is next, what is needed from them
- Explicitly ask what is working and what is not — early and directly

## Communication Cadence

Silence is the most reliable predictor of churn. It is also entirely within your control.

| Touch | Frequency | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Async update | Weekly | Short written summary: done, next, needed from you |
| Live call | Monthly | 30 minutes, results and priorities |
| Strategic review | Quarterly | QBR, see below |
| Ad-hoc wins | As they happen | Send good news immediately, do not save it for the report |

**Weekly updates go out even in a bad week.** Especially in a bad week — that is when clients start looking around. Name the problem, state the plan, give a date.

**Set response-time expectations** and meet them. "We respond within one business day" that is honored beats "always available" that is not.

## SOPs

Anything done more than twice becomes a written SOP. The test of an SOP is whether a new hire can execute it without asking a question.

Each SOP contains: trigger, owner role, step-by-step actions with the exact tools, expected output, quality checks, common failure modes, and time estimate.

Priority SOPs for a service agency: new client onboarding, access collection, monthly reporting, ad account launch, ad account weekly optimization, content production and approval, review request handling, escalation, offboarding.

**Store them where the work happens** and review quarterly. SOPs in a folder nobody opens are documentation theater.

## Quarterly Business Review

The QBR is a renewal conversation disguised as a review. Structure:

1. **Goals set last quarter** — restate them exactly as agreed
2. **Results against those goals** — honest, including misses
3. **What we learned** — including what did not work and why
4. **What is next quarter's plan** — with specific numbers
5. **What we need from you** — commitments in both directions
6. **The ask** — renewal, expansion, or referral

**Lead with the miss if there was one.** Clients trust vendors who name their own failures far more than vendors who bury them, and the miss is going to come up anyway.

## Churn Prevention

Watch for the early signals, which appear weeks before cancellation:

- Response times lengthening
- Meetings rescheduled or skipped
- The champion goes quiet or leaves the company
- Questions shift from strategy to cost
- Requests for detailed cost breakdowns
- "Can you send over everything you've done so far"

**When you see two or more, act immediately.** Call, do not email. Ask directly: "I want to check in honestly — is this working for you?" A direct question surfaces the real objection while it is still fixable.

**Structural retention levers:** results early and visibly, more than one contact inside the account, integration into their operations (living in their CRM), quarterly expansion of scope, and genuinely useful reporting.

## Offboarding

Do it well. Former clients refer, and they return.

- Export and hand over all data and assets
- Transfer account ownership rather than deleting anything
- Write a summary of what was done and what is running
- Hold an honest exit conversation and record the real reason
- Leave the door open explicitly

**Never hold accounts, domains, or data hostage.** It converts a neutral ending into an active detractor, and the reputational cost far exceeds any leverage gained.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Slow kickoff.** Momentum decays from the hour of signature.
2. **No visible deliverable in the first two weeks.** Client wonders what they are paying for.
3. **Waiting on access without daily follow-up.** Burns the first paid month.
4. **Going quiet during a bad month.** The most common cause of preventable churn.
5. **Reporting activity instead of outcomes.** "We posted 12 times" is not a result.
6. **Vague success definitions.** Guarantees a disagreement at renewal.
7. **Single point of contact.** When they leave, the account leaves.
8. **No written SOPs.** Quality varies by whoever happens to be doing the work.
9. **Hiding misses until the QBR.** Destroys trust when they surface anyway.
10. **Ignoring early churn signals.** They appear weeks ahead and are usually fixable.
11. **Hostile offboarding.** Turns a quiet exit into public damage.

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] Welcome email sent within 15 minutes of signature
- [ ] Kickoff booked within 48 hours
- [ ] Written access checklist sent, with daily follow-up until complete
- [ ] Success defined in numbers at 30, 60, and 90 days, agreed in writing
- [ ] One visible deliverable shipped within 14 days
- [ ] Weekly written updates scheduled, including during bad weeks
- [ ] Monthly call and quarterly QBR on the calendar
- [ ] Response-time expectation stated and met
- [ ] SOPs written for every repeated process, stored where the work happens
- [ ] More than one contact established inside the account
- [ ] Churn signals monitored, with a defined escalation trigger
- [ ] Offboarding process transfers all assets and records the real reason
